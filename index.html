3-5-2 Center Back Guide

A simple, expandable reference for positioning, shifting, tackling, carrying the ball, playing long, playing wide, clearing danger, and communicating as a back three.

See Decision Guide Open Match Clips
Core idea Move together. Stay compact. See the ball and your teammates. One steps. Two cover. Protect the middle first.

INTRO

What this page is for

This page is meant to be a living teaching tool. The diagrams explain the expected picture before the ball arrives. The match clips underneath can then show real examples of the same ideas: shifting together, stepping at the right moment, clearing danger, talking early, playing forward, and recovering when the ball gets behind the line.

The goal is not for every play to look identical. The goal is for the decision-making to become consistent: protect the most dangerous space, stay connected to the other center backs, and choose the safest effective action based on pressure, space, score, and game state.

POSITION EXPECTATIONS

Back-three responsibilities

LCB / RCB

Outside Center Back

  • Shift toward the ball while staying connected to the central CB.
  • Be ready to step into the channel when the wingback is beaten or pinned.
  • Do not get dragged too wide unless there is pressure and cover behind.
  • On the ball, look forward first, then wide, then recycle.
CB

Central Center Back

  • Protect the middle and organize the line.
  • Control depth when an outside CB steps.
  • Scan runners before the pass is played.
  • Communicate: “step,” “drop,” “hold,” “switch,” “runner,” and “time.”
ALL 3

Unit Behavior

  • Move together when the ball moves.
  • Keep sensible spacing — usually around 10–15 yards, adjusted for the game.
  • Protect goal side and central space first.
  • When one attacks the ball, the other two squeeze and cover.

Press or Delay?

Press harder when: the attacker takes a loose touch, receives facing his own goal or the sideline, has limited passing options, and you have cover behind you.

Delay when: the attacker is facing forward with space, you are the last defender, your covering CB is not set, or stepping would open the middle.

Cue: Read the touch. Press with a plan — otherwise delay.

Where Should You Force Him?

Usually give the outside: protect the middle, stay goal-side, and angle your approach so the attacker is encouraged toward the touchline and lower-value crossing areas.

Drive him toward help: if your wingback or covering CB is set, funnel the attacker into that teammate rather than allowing a free inside dribble.

Cue: Outside is safer. Protect the middle and use your teammate.

1v1 Body Shape & Who Moves First

Your shape: side-on, knees bent, balanced, small quick steps, hips open enough to see both the ball and the runner. Do not stand square or cross your feet.

Make the attacker move first: close the space, then slow down. Hold a distance where you can react. Use your angle and patience to make him commit before you tackle.

Force the bad touch: crowd his preferred route, show him toward the sideline or weaker foot, and accelerate only when the touch gets away from him.

Cue: Fast to arrive. Slow to defend. Make him choose first.

Blocking Shots

Clues the shot is coming: the attacker gets the ball out of his feet, looks up at goal, opens his body, slows to set himself, or has no obvious pass available.

When to attack the block: close the distance while he is setting the ball, stay balanced until the shooting motion begins, then make yourself big without turning away.

Technique: get low, protect your face/body, keep arms close, lead with the nearest leg when appropriate, and stay on your feet whenever possible.

Cue: Close the angle. Stay brave. Block the shot — don't turn away.

DECISION GUIDE

What should the center back do?

Use these pictures as a quick decision framework. They are not rigid rules; they show the cues that usually make one option safer than another.

Go & Tackle

Go when: the receiver has a poor touch, receives with back to goal, has no easy escape, and you have cover behind you.

Do not dive in when: the attacker is facing forward with space, your covering CB is not set, or leaving your line opens the middle.

Cue: Arrive under control. Win it or delay it.

Dribble / Carry Forward

Carry when: nobody steps to you and open space is available in front. Drive forward enough to force a midfielder to leave someone.

Release the ball when: pressure arrives, the passing lane opens, or carrying farther would expose the team if possession is lost.

Cue: Carry to attract — then pass.

Play Long

Play long when: the opponent has compressed midfield, the forward has a clear matchup/run, or pressure makes a short pass risky.

Best long ball: purposeful, into a target, channel, or space the team can attack — not simply an uncontrolled clearance.

Cue: Long with a reason, not long from panic.

Play Wide

Play wide when: the wingback has space, the opponent is narrow, or the pass moves pressure away from the center.

After the pass: support underneath, adjust your body position, and prepare for the next pass or immediate counterpress.

Cue: Use width to stretch pressure.

CLEARING & COMMUNICATION

Simple match-day rules

Clearing danger

  • If you can play out safely, play out.
  • If the box is crowded and the ball is bouncing, remove danger first.
  • Clear high and wide when possible — not straight back into the middle.
  • After the clearance, push the line out together instead of watching the ball.

Communication examples

STEP — attack the receiver. DROP — protect space behind. HOLD — don't get pulled out. LEFT / RIGHT — shift the unit. RUNNER — identify movement behind. TIME — teammate can turn or settle. MAN ON — pressure is arriving. AWAY — clear the danger.
